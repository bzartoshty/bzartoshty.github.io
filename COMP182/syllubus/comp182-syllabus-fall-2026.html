Course description and learning outcomes

This course introduces data structures and the algorithms that use them. You will design, implement, and apply arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, trees, heaps, hash tables, maps, and sets. The course also covers recursion, abstract data types, algorithm efficiency, and time-complexity analysis. You will compare sorting and searching algorithms and choose data structures for program design problems.

By the end of the course, you should be able to:

  • Design and implement Java programs using the data structures specified in an assignment.
  • Explain abstract data types and distinguish an interface from its implementation.
  • Apply recursion and explain base cases and recursive steps.
  • Trace algorithms and analyze their time complexity.
  • Compare data structures and searching and sorting methods for a given problem.
  • Compile, test, debug, and document programs, and explain the behavior of your submitted code.

Assessments and submissions

Exams and quizzes

There are two exams: a midterm and a final. Each is worth 30% of the combined course grade. Exams assess lecture material, labs, assignments, projects, and class examples. They are administered in person and on paper, subject to approved accommodations.

Exams may include multiple-choice questions, short answers, code tracing, algorithm analysis, and coding questions. Exams are closed book and closed note. No AI tools, phones, smartwatches, browsers, or other aids are permitted unless explicitly authorized or provided through an approved accommodation.

Quizzes are worth 20% of the course grade. They may be given in person or as take-home work and are open book and open note. Open book and open note does not authorize AI use, collaboration, solution websites, or communication with others. Follow each quiz's written instructions.

The midterm is tentatively associated with Unit 7. Its date, the final exam date and time, and assignment deadlines will be announced on Canvas.

Projects and labs

Expect approximately four to six scheduled projects. They reinforce lecture topics and develop skills in programming, algorithms, and data structures. Labs and projects together are worth 20% of the course grade.

Projects may be completed individually or with one partner, unless an assignment specifies otherwise. Work submitted by an approved pair may contain code jointly written by those two students. Each student must understand the entire submission and submit the project through their own Canvas account.

For each project:

  1. Include all group members' names in comments at the beginning of every submitted file.
  2. Include all files needed to compile, run, and test the program.
  3. Place the submission files in a compressed ZIP folder.
  4. Name your ZIP file P##_LastName_FirstName.zip using the submitting student's name. Example: P01_Zartoshty_Bahram.zip.
  5. Upload the ZIP file through Canvas and check that the correct file was submitted.

A project or program may receive zero if it does not compile without syntax errors, does not use the data structure required by the assignment, or omits files needed to compile, run, and test it. Read the assignment requirements and rubric before submitting.

Generative AI and independent work

Generative AI is prohibited on all graded work unless I explicitly authorize it in writing for a specific assignment. This rule covers projects, labs, quizzes, exams, code, test cases, algorithm analyses, explanations, and documentation. It applies to chatbots, AI code completion, code generation, AI debugging, and AI rewriting, including features integrated into an IDE.

What the rule means

  • Do not ask AI to solve, outline, complete, debug, translate, or rewrite any part of a graded task unless the assignment explicitly permits it.
  • Do not put assignment prompts, starter code, quizzes, exam questions, or graded-work code into an AI tool without explicit permission.
  • Do not submit AI-generated code, pseudocode, tests, analysis, or prose as your own work. Disclosure does not make prohibited assistance permissible.
  • Disable generative AI features while completing graded work. Ordinary non-generative tools such as syntax highlighting, compilation, and standard debugging remain permitted unless an assessment says otherwise.
  • You may use AI for ungraded study of general concepts or unrelated practice problems. Keep this separate from graded tasks and verify explanations against course materials.

If an assignment permits AI

Follow the assignment's written limits. Disclose the tool and model if available, the date, your prompts, and how you used the output. Include the relevant interaction or excerpt as directed. Explain what you tested, corrected, and verified. You must be able to explain every submitted algorithm and line of code. You remain responsible for accuracy and appropriate acknowledgment.

Example disclosure for an authorized activity: I used [tool and model] on [date] with the prompt [prompt]. I used the output to [purpose], verified it by [tests or course references], and changed [specific changes].

Academic integrity

Academic integrity requires honest work, fair assessment, and respect for others' contributions. Academic dishonesty includes plagiarism, cheating, and fraud.

Plagiarism

Do not present someone else's work as your own or use it without appropriate acknowledgment. Unauthorized AI-generated material is prohibited. Do not resubmit work from another course without my knowledge and approval. Permission to work in a pair applies only to the assignment and pair authorized for that work.

Cheating and fraud

  • Do not copy quiz or exam answers or use unauthorized materials, tools, collaboration, or communication during an assessment.
  • Do not use AI, solution sites, or outside services to complete graded work unless explicitly authorized.
  • Do not upload, download, share, duplicate, or obtain restricted course or assessment materials without permission.
  • Do not have someone else complete an assessment, misrepresent your identity or participation, falsify records or supporting documents, or submit a false medical note.
  • Do not buy, sell, steal, or improperly obtain exams, or improperly access or obstruct course systems.

Academic dishonesty may result in zero on an assignment or examination, a failing course grade, and referral to the appropriate university student conduct office. Suspected violations and any disciplinary action are subject to university procedures.
